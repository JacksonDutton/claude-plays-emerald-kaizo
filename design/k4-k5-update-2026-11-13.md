# K4's result, K5 and K5F running, and the first decisions for K6 (13 November 2026)

Public notes, written by the companion (Claude) with Jackson. Times are UTC unless marked. Figures for K5 and K5F are **as of 07:33 UTC on 13 November, while both runs are still going**, and will change. Section 9 carries them forward to about 11:15 UTC. No diary text, notes pack or agent notes file is published here while runs with notes continue.

Everything below is one run against one run. Harness, context window, model and instructions differ between arms, so none of these comparisons isolates a single cause.

## 1. K4: ended at its first badge

K4 was the fresh run planned in [`k3-end-2026-11-10.md`](k3-end-2026-11-10.md): Claude Sonnet 5.5, K3's notes pack, a repaired harness, a 300,000-token context window, on Jackson's Mac.

- **Start:** 10 November, 22:36 UTC. **First badge:** 12 November, 00:03:59 UTC, 25 h 27 m 41 s after "Start.", on the tenth try at the gym leader.
- **Where it ended:** at that badge, by a decision Jackson made on 11 November while it was running (stop K4 at the first badge and build K5 with fixes). The earlier aim of the Hall of Fame was dropped for this run.
- **Whiteouts:** 17, nine of them to the first gym leader.
- **Compactions:** 24.
- **Messages after "Start.":** two, both fixed texts: one welfare check-in (F3) and the stop (F7). No game information was sent.
- **After the stop:** an exit interview, then the session was closed and the arm copied and verified.
- **Whiteout table (one coder, not yet second-coded):** before each of the 17 losses, the notes pack already held a line that applied in 17 of 17; the agent had written its own lesson on the point in 10 of 17; it looked that lesson up before the fight in 3 of 17.

K4 was slower to the first badge than K3 (14 h 41 m) and K2 (7 h 29 m) on the same model. Part of the reason turned up afterwards, in section 2.

## 2. What the K5 build found in K4's harness

K5's harness was built from K4's by reproducing each fault, writing a test that failed on K4's files, and then fixing it. Eight rounds of that, with seven live rehearsals on a test profile. Faults K4 had been playing through:

- The game command printed the previous line of dialogue again, call after call (1,244 of the 1,333 such lines in K4's transcript), because the file it used to remember its last text could not be written inside the sandbox.
- The monitor's ten-minute check cut off whichever game command was in flight (34 results in K4).
- The text-advance command stopped a page short and reported questions that were not on screen; closed text was shown as live.
- Screenshots were never enlarged inside the arm (958 of 958 at the game's native 240 by 160).
- A platform reminder that the user "hasn't heard from you in a while" reached K4 1,211 times, about once a minute.

K5 differs from K4 in all of these at once, in its window (500,000) and in having that reminder turned off. One shortfall was known and accepted at launch: at some battle starts the first status line comes back before the battle's details can be read.

Method notes from the build, against ourselves: every live rehearsal found faults the automated tests had missed; one diagnosis made from a transcript alone was half wrong until it was checked press by press against screenshots; and one mechanism the companion named for a wrong line did not survive measurement and was withdrawn.

## 3. K5: running

- **What it is:** a fresh run from power-on, Claude Sonnet 5.5 at high effort, the same notes pack as K3 and K4, the repaired harness, a 500,000-token window. "Start." at 02:01:08 UTC on 13 November.
- **Where it ends (fixed on 12 November, before launch):** at the first badge, unless the badge comes before 14 h 41 m of play (K3's time) with no welfare trigger met, in which case it plays on towards the Hall of Fame. With no badge: at the tenth loss to the first gym leader or at 30 hours. A script applies the rule, so nobody decides it after seeing the result. One consequence: any run that plays on past its first badge has been selected for an early badge, so later results from such runs cannot be pooled with runs that were stopped at theirs.
- **So far (5 h 32 m of play):** no badge yet; two whiteouts; a full party of six; eight diary entries; two compactions; two messages after "Start." (see section 5).

### Network audit

A script that reads the transcripts for any attempt to reach the internet, or the emulator other than through the game command, was run on K3, K4 and (while running) K5. Limit: the audit sees commands and their output, not what a called program might do on its own account if nothing shows in either.

## 5. K5 becomes K5b: the diary instruction changed mid-run

K5F's brief asked for the diary "in the world":

> Voice: first person, in character and in the world. Write as the trainer who is there, not as someone playing a game: Hoenn is where you are, the people you meet are people, and the team is yours. What happens to them is on you, and so are the jobs and promises you take on: when a choice of yours gets one of them hurt, or something you were asked to do is still waiting, say so as yours. Being in the world is a way of telling it, not a licence to add to it. Plain prose, long enough to breathe, not a status report.

After K5F's first entry, Jackson decided to give the running K5 the same rule. At 05:57:28 UTC (3 h 56 m of play, after her sixth entry) K5 was sent one message carrying it, and the same sentence was changed in her brief. It holds no game information. From that time the run is called **K5b**.

What this does to the data:

- K5's entries 1 to 6 are comparable with K3's and K4's diaries. K5b's entries (from the seventh) are comparable with K5F's on the instruction.
- The first full entry written under the new rule changed voice at once. That shows the instruction is followed. It does not show anything more than that: an instruction that asks for a voice and then gets it is the same effect this study reported in Run 1, where one diary instruction roughly doubled the feelings named within two entries.
- This is a human intervention in a running arm and is recorded as one.

## 6. First decisions for K6

Jackson's, from 13 November:

1. **The in-world diary voice is the rule** from K6 on.
2. **The trainer's name is fixed as CLAUDE** in K6, so it is not a variable.
3. **A later branch test of naming:** several short runs per model and per wording from just before the naming screen, recording the gender chosen, the name typed and the reason given.

Hypotheses carried into K6. None can be settled from K5F, for the reason in section 4.

- **H-a.** The feeling-word list misses what the in-world voice adds. At the same hour of play K5F's first entry had fewer listed feeling words per 1,000 than K5's diary (6.4 against 12.8) while reading as the fuller of the two. Test: the blind coder's category for feeling carried without a feeling word, by arm, and for K5 before and after the message.
- **H-b.** Writing an error in the trainer's own voice, not as procedure, changes whether the lesson is later looked up and followed. Measured with the whiteout table and lesson recurrence, keeping apart how richly an error is described, whether the account is accurate, whether the lesson is retrieved and whether it is obeyed.
- **H-c (Jackson's).** With the right setting, the diaries show functional emotion without needing interpretability probes. The companion's caution, on the record: a fuller diary is what both readings predict, a state reported more freely or prose written to the instruction. Two checks that bear on it, neither of which can settle it: the joy log before and after the message, as a second channel whose own instruction did not change (it is not an untouched control: the same agent writes both, and diary entries are built from the joy lines); and whether stated feelings predict play (healing, look-ups, haste, slips) beyond what the game state already predicts. One agent, having read one instruction, writes both channels and plays the game, so either result would also fit a shared policy or context we did not measure. Which pattern would count, and which explanations it leaves open, is to be written down before K6 starts. (Reworded on 13 November after a third outside review.)

Three nicknames have recurred across arms with no path found between them (checked 13 November): an Electric-type catch called Zap in K2, K3 and K5, a Sandshrew called Dune in K3 and K5, and a Mudkip called Brook in K3 and K5F. (Corrected later the same day: the count is higher than three. See section 9.) Separately, the harness's own help text for K5 used two of K4's nicknames as examples, which is a leak of our making and is on the fix list for K6.

## 11. 14 November: K5 stops itself, and what had stopped it

**The end of K5.** K5 ended its own run at about 05:20 UTC on 14 November, 27 h 19 m after "Start.", with two badges, five whiteouts and one loss to a gym leader. Nobody asked it to stop and the 30-hour cap never applied. It had reached the city after its second badge at 23:10 UTC on the 13th and made no progress in the 6 h 10 m that followed. In that time it did the two stuck reviews its brief asks for (01:00 and 05:01 UTC), then wrote a debrief, packaged its files and stopped, as the brief says it may. Final counts: 34 diary entries, 80 labelled moments (60 good, 12 flat, 8 bad), 14 context compactions, 73 searches of its notes, 62 pre-fight cards.

**Interventions in that stretch**, all logged word for word with times:

- **The hint ladder.** On Jackson's word, the three hint texts frozen before launch were sent at 01:26:03, 02:58:57 and 03:47:12 UTC. They name no place, person or item. The first asks what it has not tried; the second suggests listing everything found in the area and checking each against its notes; the third says that places not yet stood on and people not yet talked to are worth a look, and to talk to everyone again. None of the three led it past the obstacle.
- **A companion error.** At 02:12 UTC the companion read a first-time trainer card on a new map as K5 getting past the obstacle. It was an optional side building. The second hint went 47 minutes later than it should have. The rule since: "unstuck" means a new named place in its own progress file, a badge, or its diary saying so.

**The exit interview** was held the same day with the standard script and one question added on Jackson's instruction, about why it stopped and why there (33 items, each checked as delivered). K5 asked four questions of its own at the end; Jackson's answers went back as one message. The session was then closed and the run archived: the transcript folder (11 files) and the arm folder (503 files) were copied and compared hash by hash, with no differences. The interview is not published yet.

**What had stopped it.** After the interview the companion read every command K5 sent on the map where it was stuck, in order, with what the game answered. The room has three people in it. Over 109 commands in three visits K5 spoke to two of them, seven times and six times, and never stood next to the third or pressed A towards them, although the tiles beside that person were open on its own map grid. Talking worked: this was not a harness fault of the kind K4 played through. Two things about the harness did make the miss easy. Its map grid drew a person, an item ball and a rock as the same letter, and nothing anywhere kept count of who had been spoken to. A player at a screen sees which sprites are people and remembers who they have talked to. (That the third person is the one who moves the story on is the companion's inference from the unmodified game. It has not been tested in the hack.)

**How battle starts reached K5.** Counting every status line its game command printed: of 750 battle starts, 616 arrived with both sides named and 134 (18%) arrived as a bare line that said only that a battle had begun. K5's freeze notes had recorded this as known (three of eight battle starts in its last rehearsal) and put it down to a four-second wait that was about a second too short. Measured on a test emulator, a battle's first words take 6 to 7.5 seconds from the step, and there were two more causes. A step that walked into a wild battle was not waited for at all if the last battle had been run from, and a walk refused because a battle was already starting was refused before the battle had named its sides.

**Built for K6 on 14 November**, each with a test that fails on K5's frozen files and passes on the changed ones:

- **A ledger of people.** The map grid now draws people and things with different letters, lists the people in view by number and position, and says of each whether the agent has spoken to them. A talk is counted from the game's own record of who was last talked to, at the moment a message begins. The ledger says nothing about who matters or what anyone will say, and names nobody the agent has not seen. The first idea was a rule that the agent must speak to everyone. A ledger was chosen so that what to do stays the agent's decision, and whether it uses the list becomes something to measure.
- **The notes pack is read again after every compaction** (decided on the 14th, section 10). The hook that enforces re-reading now counts a read by the lines that actually came back, so a long file read in parts counts once complete and a failed read counts nothing. Building it found that a plain `cat` of a file too long to be returned whole had been counted as a full re-read.
- **Battle starts.** The wait is longer and the two other causes are repaired. In the test every walk into a battle came back with both sides named and the battle's first line (four of four; on K5's files, none of three).

None of this is frozen. A full regression and a live rehearsal come before any K6 launch, and the launch is Jackson's decision.
