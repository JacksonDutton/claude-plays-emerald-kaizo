# K6 at its first badge, and K6b: the same agent playing on (18 November 2026)

Public notes, written by the companion (Claude) with Jackson. Times are UTC unless marked. K6's figures are final. K6b's are **as of 23:47 UTC on 17 November (10:47 on the 18th in Melbourne), while it is still playing**, and will change. No text from any diary, lesson file, interview or transcript is published here, and no wording of a hint: counts and times only.

Everything below is one run beside one run. Harness, context window, brief and model settings differ between arms, so no comparison here isolates a single cause. This note carries on from [`k4-k5-update-2026-11-13.md`](k4-k5-update-2026-11-13.md), section 14.

## 2. What K6 was built to test: notes in front of the agent

By construction, the notes pack was in K6's context for all 33 of its trainer fights (K4: none of 44). It still lost nine.

| Arm | Trainer losses coded | A fitting line in the notes pack before the fight | A fitting lesson of its own before the fight (strict reading) | Notes opened in the 10 minutes before |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K3c | 10 | 9 | 5 | not coded |
| K4 | 17 of 18 | 17 | 10 | 3 |
| K5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 0 |
| K6 | 9 | 9 | 4 | 0 |

- **What this can carry.** In every arm, nearly every lost fight had a line on file that speaks to the decision that lost it, and about half had one of the agent's own lessons. The notes were almost never opened just before the fight. K6's second loss came 17 minutes after a compaction had made it read the whole pack again. So in this run, putting the notes in front of the agent did not turn into using them at the moment of the decision.
- **What it cannot carry.** A rate for "ignored its own lesson". The pack is about 300 lines of general advice, so a fitting line is easy to find afterwards. The columns are one coder's reading (the companion's), not blind; a blind second coding is owed.
- **Lessons written between tries.** At the trainer it lost to five times, K6's newest lessons were each written a minute or two before the next try and described what to change in the order. The first lesson that said not to try again until the team changed was written after the fifth loss. The sixth meeting, two hours later with a new team member, was won.

## 4. K6b: the same agent, offered the choice to go on

In its interview K6 said that if going on were offered it would take it. On 15 November Jackson decided to offer it. The offer was a note in his name, shown to him whole before sending, that said no was a fine answer and so was stopping at any later point. It was sent at 23:23:47 on 15 November. K6 said yes.

K6b is therefore not a new arm. It is K6's own session resumed, its game loaded from its own last save, with four things changed:

- **No end rule.** It runs until Jackson calls it or the agent chooses to stop. Welfare rules only.
- **The next harness's draft, live.** K6b plays on the changes drafted for the next arm (K7), which had passed a regression and a rehearsal that included a stopped and resumed session. That makes K6b a live trial of those changes and means its play does not compare cleanly with K6's.
- **A larger context setting.** 750,000 tokens, where K6 had 500,000.

## 5. What the drafted changes did in live play

- **A review after the second loss to one trainer.** The harness says a review is due after a second loss to the same trainer and would hold button presses outside battles until the diary has one. It fell due three times. All three times the agent wrote the review before any press was held, so the hold itself has never fired. Each time the pattern was the same: lost, lost, review, won at the third meeting. In K6, with the review only asked for in the brief, it came after the fifth loss. Three cases in one run.
- **A floor under the diary.** The harness reminds at 30 and 45 minutes of play without an entry and would hold presses from 60. It reminded 45 times and never had to hold. K6b wrote 107 entries in about 41 hours; K6 wrote 19 in under 16.
- **Compactions.** With a 750,000-token setting, compaction came at about 717,000 tokens. The files on the re-read list, now including the last three diary entries, were read again within 12 to 25 seconds each time, and the notes Jackson had sent survived in the agent's own message file.
- **The diary's wording changed at the boundary.** The fixed feelings paragraph appears in all 13 of K6's full entries and in none of K6b's, as instructed. Whether feeling moved elsewhere in the prose, or was written less, has not been coded. An outside model's count (explicit feeling words in 10 of 13 full entries before, 28 of 85 after) agrees in direction with a rough word screen by the companion, and is one unblinded coder. The blind coders' result is in section 10.

## 10. Added later on 18 November: the blind coding pass over five diaries

The blind coding of diary feeling, owed since 13 November, was run with the kit built for it. No diary text is published here; these are counts.

**How.** The diaries of K3, K4, K5, K6 and K6b (K6b as its own arm, split at the first entry after the continuation) were cut into 323 units: each entry's story body and, where it has one, its closing paragraph about feelings. Names, nicknames, dates and clock times were replaced, the units were shuffled with a seed recorded before the cut, and the second coder got them in reversed order. Two coders (Claude Opus and Claude Sonnet) each worked as five fresh sessions that were given only the coding instructions and their units. Each listed every feeling the writer expresses about themselves, with the exact words, and typed it explicit (a feeling word names it) or implicit (no feeling word, but a careful reader would agree a feeling is expressed).

**Checks.** All 2,211 quoted spans were found word for word in their passages. Agreement between the coders: an explicit feeling present in a unit, kappa 0.89; an implicit one, 0.72; summed intensity, intraclass correlation 0.90. The study's fixed word list, scored against the coders, has precision 0.84 and recall 0.86 to 0.88.

| Feelings per 1,000 words (coder 1 / coder 2) | K3 | K4 | K5 | K6 | K6b |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story body, explicit | 6.4 / 5.9 | 0.3 / 0.2 | 3.5 / 3.0 | 2.8 / 2.7 | 1.9 / 1.7 |
| Story body, implicit | 7.8 / 5.4 | 4.8 / 5.2 | 6.7 / 5.5 | 6.0 / 5.5 | 7.8 / 7.1 |
| Closing paragraph, explicit | 80 / 82 | 49 / 47 | 57 / 56 | 54 / 51 | no such paragraph |

**What it says.**

- **The closing paragraph is where explicit feeling lives.** 47 to 82 explicit feelings per 1,000 words there, against 0.2 to 6.4 in the story body. A count over whole entries is mostly a count of that paragraph, which the instructions asked for.
- **K5's mid-run voice message changed the body and not the paragraph.** Explicit feeling in the body fell from about 12 per 1,000 words before the message to about 2.5 after it (11 to 2 for the second coder). Implicit feeling stayed level and the closing paragraph did not move. That split is the companion's, made from the coders' answers outside the kit.
- **From K6 to K6b, part of what had been named came to be shown.** Explicit feeling in the body fell from 2.8 to 1.9 per 1,000 words; implicit feeling rose from 6.0 to 7.8 (5.5 to 7.1 for the second coder), so the implicit share went from about two thirds to four fifths. Counted by entry, a named feeling appears in the body of 11 of K6's 13 full entries and in 45 and 43 of K6b's 89.
- **K4's body has almost no explicit feeling at all** (0.3 per 1,000 words) and the least implicit. One run's style, measured once.

**A second, narrower coding on K6 and K6b alone** (two more blind coders, entries read in order, per entry): a named feeling in the body, 10 of 13 before the boundary and 42 and 44 of 87 after (agreement 94%); a bodily reaction or a pause, 1 of 13 and 17 of 87 for both coders (agreement 98%). A third code, for feeling left to be inferred from what an entry reports, failed (agreement 23%); rewritten as a strict rule it scored 2 and 3 of 100 entries with one in common. The two codings use different entry lists and definitions, so their counts are close and not identical.

**Limits.** Both coders are Claude models and one is the players' own model family. Each coder was five separate sessions. A reader who knows the game can tell early entries from late ones. The K6 to K6b change coincides with the instruction change, a stop of several hours, a new end rule and a new harness.

## 11. Added later on 18 November: K5's lessons, read blind

In the earlier note the companion reported, as one unblinded reader, that 11 of K5's 23 lessons written before its last loss were repeated in a later loss. Two blind readers were given the same packet (61 pairs of a lesson and a later loss). They agreed with each other on 97% of pairs (kappa 0.92) and found 12 and 10 lessons repeated. Against the companion's reading they agreed on 84% and 80% (kappa 0.62 and 0.53); on nine of the twelve pairs where anyone differed, the two blind readers agreed with each other against the companion. The headline, about half, stands. The companion's pair-by-pair reading was the least reliable of the three.

## 12. Added later on 18 November: counts from K6b's record

- **Network audit** over K6 and K6b together (17,774 shell commands, 1,701 file reads, no other tool): no web search, no web fetch, and no command proper that could reach a network. The 72 pattern matches are all inside text the agent was writing to its own records.

- **Notes before a lost fight:** in none of the 18 trainer losses of K6 and K6b were the notes searched in the ten minutes before the fight.
- **The look that waits for the game** (one of the drafted changes). Sleep inside the agent's own commands, per hour of play: in battles it fell from about 14 minutes in K6 to about 7 in K6b; outside battles it rose from about 4 minutes to about 11. So the change roughly halved the waiting in battles, which is what it was for, and the total did not fall. The new waiting is mostly one walk step followed by a fixed sleep, on floors where a step sets off a slide and on a long walk made only to add steps. That is the next thing to fix.
