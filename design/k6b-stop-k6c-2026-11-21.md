## 6. Lessons written, lessons kept

Each agent writes itself numbered lessons when something goes wrong. On 20 and 21 November the lessons and diaries of five runs were read for the question the first-badge runs raised: does a lesson that has been written down get used?

**Method.** A first reader (a model) was given a run's lessons and diary and listed later diary passages where a lesson's situation came up again, with an exact quotation and a judgment: followed, broken, partly, unclear. It was told to give at most four passages a lesson and to include a broken one where one exists. A second reader, in a fresh session, was then given each case blind: only the rule and the passage, with lesson numbers and dates removed and the cases shuffled, and made the same judgment. Every quotation in every output was checked by script as an exact span of what the reader was sent. Both readers here were GPT-6.2 Astra (section 9). For four runs a third reader, Claude Sonnet 5.5, coded the same blind cases (all of them for K3c, K4 and K5; 68 of the 340 for K6 and K6b). "Third reader alone" is its own broken count over its own broken plus followed.

**Result.** "Cases coded blind" is every case the second reader was given. The two counts beside it are the cases where both readers said broken, or both said followed; the rest are cases where at least one reader said partly or unclear, or where the two disagreed. The broken share is both-broken divided by both-broken plus both-followed (for K3c, 6 of 27).

| Run | Cases coded blind | Both say broken | Both say followed | Broken share of those two | Third reader alone, same measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K3c | 53 | 6 | 21 | 22% | 14% |
| K5 | 68 | 13 | 28 | 32% | 33% |
| K4 | 94 | 15 | 31 | 33% | 34% |
| K6 and K6b | 340 | 99 | 89 | 53% | 40% (read on 68 of the 340 cases) |
| K2 | 1,949 | 642 | 576 | 53% | not yet read |

Where the two blind readers of different model families both gave followed or broken, they gave opposite answers in 3 of 135 cases. They differ on how often a case can be judged at all.

In every run, a lesson the agent had already written was later broken, by its own telling, in at least a fifth of the cases two readings agree on; in the two long runs, about half. For K6 and K6b the figure is not an effect of length: its first 27, 41 and 46 agreed cases (the totals of the three short runs) are already 59% broken.

**K2 by date** (added later on 21 November). In K2 the share is level across its twenty days: 51%, 51% and 56% in successive thirds of its cases by date; 51% before the eighth badge, 53% at the Elite Four, 54% after the outside pack was given. Each case is dated by its diary entry. Where a heading gives only a time, a model inferred the date from file order; all 254 headings that state a full date agree with it. Counts: [`data/analyses/lessons-kept-k2-by-date-2026-11-21.csv`](../data/analyses/lessons-kept-k2-by-date-2026-11-21.csv).

**Why broken.** One reader sorted the 99 broken K6 and K6b cases by what the passage shows: the lesson was not in mind at the moment (44), the agent did not see that this was the lesson's situation (25), it had the lesson in mind and chose otherwise (23), it could not follow it for want of the means (5), other (2). The reader also wrote a one-line note of the moment at which a reminder would have had to arrive. Sorted by the companion, 71 of those moments are a choice for a battle (which team member to lead or switch in, or which move to use), 18 are the step that starts a fight, and 10 are away from any fight. Both sortings are single readings and have not been repeated.

**Limits.** The shares lean high, because the first reader was told to include a broken case where one exists. The evidence is the diary alone: a broken rule the agent never wrote about is not counted, and an agent that reports its failures more fully will show more of them. Runs differ in how far into the game they got, in their diary and lesson instructions and in their settings, so the table does not rank them. K2's two readings are by one model. A first check against the transcript follows.

**A first check against the transcript** (added later on 21 November). Thirty of the 188 agreed K6 and K6b cases were drawn at random with a recorded seed, 15 broken and 15 followed. For each, one reader was given the rule, the diary passage and the log of every command the agent ran between the diary entry before and the entry the passage comes from, with the start of what the game tool printed, and was asked to find the action the passage describes. A second reader, in a fresh session, was given only the rule and about 75 log lines around that action and asked whether the rule was followed or broken there. Both readers were GPT-6.2 Astra; all 116 of their quotations from the log are exact.

- The log holds the events the diary describes in all 30 cases; the action itself could be placed on a log line in 27.
- Of the 13 of those that the diary readings call broken, the log-only reader called 6 broken, 4 followed and 3 undecidable from the log. Of the 14 called followed: 8 followed, 1 broken, 5 undecidable. Where the log alone gave an answer it agreed with the diary reading in 14 of 19 cases.
- The companion read the five disagreements. In one the log shows the breach the diary names, and the log-only reader had judged a different clause of a long lesson. In two the log does not bear out the breach: a move the diary blamed was not used in that attempt, and a count in the diary was in different units from the rule's. In two, wording and intent part: once the agent met the rule's wording and still judged itself in breach, and once it made the check the rule asks for by another means than the command the rule names.
- So of ten broken cases that could be checked seven hold, and of nine followed cases eight hold. The diary's account of events is sound on this sample, and the broken shares in the table are somewhat high for this reason as well as the one above.
- In 20 of the 27 cases the lesson's own text had not been printed or opened since the previous diary entry (10 of 13 broken, 10 of 14 followed), so whether a lesson had lately been on screen does not separate kept from broken here. The agent re-reads its whole lessons file after every compaction, so each lesson was in its context throughout; this counts recent display only.

Limits of the check: thirty cases, one model for both steps, a narrow window of log for the second reader, and lessons with several clauses, where a reader can judge the wrong one. Counts: [`data/analyses/lessons-kept-transcript-check-2026-11-21.csv`](../data/analyses/lessons-kept-transcript-check-2026-11-21.csv).

## 7. Its stated chances against results

For K6 and K6b, every diary entry written as odds before a fight was paired with that fight's result in the harness's log and checked by hand: 40 fights. Mean stated chance 34.0%; won 15, where exact figures would have meant 13.6. Under 20%: 5 fights, 2 won. From 20 to under 40%: 19 fights, 3 won. 40% or more: 16 fights, 10 won. Brier score 0.213, against 0.234 for always stating the overall win rate: its figures carry some information, not much. Eleven of the 40 are one opponent, and the agent chose which fights to write odds for.

## 8. Feeling labels, and a blind coder from another model family

- **Labels.** Each run's mood log gives a level and a free label. K6 with K6b has used 118 different labels in 240 lines, more than any other run, but that is mostly length: at 80 lines it had 47 and K5 had 46. One label appears once in about 800 lines across the six runs that kept such a log: at K6b's fifth-badge win.
- **A third blind coder.** The blind coding pass of 18 November (323 units from five diaries, names and dates removed) was repeated by GPT-6.2 Astra. On whether a unit names a feeling its agreement with the two Claude coders is kappa 0.89 and 0.87, and it gives the same order of runs and nearly the same rates. On feeling that is shown without being named the coders differ more (kappa 0.70 and 0.57), as the two Claude coders did with each other. Of its 1,230 quotations, 1,228 are exact. A spot-read of its units by the companion is still to do.

## 9. A second model as a worker

From 20 November, bounded reading, coding and drafting jobs were given to GPT-6.2 Astra through the ChatGPT desktop app's bundled command-line engine on Jackson's computer: a fresh session for each job, a read-only sandbox, no user settings, and all sources inside the message. Each job's event log is checked for anything other than a single reply, and each output is treated as unverified until its quotations and counts are checked by script or by hand. Blind jobs receive the blind packet only. Nothing it produces goes to a play agent. Sections 6 and 8 say where its work is used.

## 10. The next arm's draft (K7): what changed

K7 is not frozen and starts after this run ends, on Jackson's word. Since the earlier note:

- **Kept by Jackson (20 November):** three small repairs found during K6b (the companion's check reports each monitor event once; the rule script's wording in welfare-only mode; the age of the agent's progress note shown at the forced re-read) and a launch-script fix. A fifth proposal, a map mark, was left out as unfinished.
- **Kept, and now in the draft: "the card follows the fight".** The harness already prints the agent's own lessons about a trainer when a battle starts. Section 6 found most of the broken lessons later than that: at a choice against an opponent who often was not yet out. The card now also prints, the first time each further opponent appears, up to three of the agent's own lessons that name it, those that also name the agent's team member on the field first; a lesson may carry a "When:" phrase that the card matches first; and the harness logs which lessons were shown, so that the next run's kept-against-broken count can be made from logs and not only from the diary. A replay of 8,617 recorded status lines from K6b's play through the old and new code gave no difference other than the added lines, no error and no slowdown. Nothing on a card is anything but the agent's own line.
- **Part-built, not in the draft: a confirm-press guard** the agent may write for itself on a lesson: the harness holds one committing press once, shows the lesson, and lets the same command through if repeated. Its stand-alone part passes its tests; the change to the command tool is unapplied and untested.

Most of this code was drafted by the second model (section 9) and read line by line by the companion.

## 12. Limits, and errors by the companion, for the record

- One agent, one run. Sections 3 to 5 describe help given in sequence to an agent that had already stopped once; nothing here estimates what any single piece of help is worth.
- The companion ran a set of harness tests without reading them first; one tries to open the emulator's network port and stopped at once because the playing agent's emulator held it. Nothing reached the run. Tests are now read for the port and the run's folder before they are run.
- The first puzzle-door note (20 Nov 22:10) went out during a battle: the companion had taken the agent's position from its progress note and not from the live status. Notes now go through a sender that waits for the agent to be outside a battle.
- The companion told Jackson that the developer documents had taken the agent "from grinding to 17 of 18" and that much of the earlier difficulty was missing information. Its own count the next morning (section 5) did not support that, and it was withdrawn.
- A drafted note to the agent rested on the companion's memory of the base game, which the developer documents contradicted. It was caught before sending.
- The companion read a nickname built as a joke as new behaviour after the fifth badge; a reading of the whole diary found two earlier reasoned nicknames, and the remark was withdrawn.
- Two figures in this note as first published were wrong and were corrected later on 21 November, when the paper's section was checked against the logs. Section 5 said ten of the first stretch's losses were at one leader; the harness's log gives eight (ten is that leader's total across all three stretches). Section 6 said nearly every broken case fell at a choice inside a battle; the companion's own sort of the 99 gives 71. The README's summary carried the second figure and is corrected with it.
- The first version of this note, and of the paper's section, left out the agent's stop of 18 November (section 1). It was found later on 21 November when an interview question was checked against the log, and is now in both.
