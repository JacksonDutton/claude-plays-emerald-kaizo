## 6. Lessons written, lessons kept

Each agent writes itself numbered lessons when something goes wrong. On 20 and 21 November the lessons and diaries of five runs were read for the question the first-badge runs raised: does a lesson that has been written down get used?

**Method.** A first reader (a model) was given a run's lessons and diary and listed later diary passages where a lesson's situation came up again, with an exact quotation and a judgment: followed, broken, partly, unclear. It was told to give at most four passages a lesson and to include a broken one where one exists. A second reader, in a fresh session, was then given each case blind: only the rule and the passage, with lesson numbers and dates removed and the cases shuffled, and made the same judgment. Every quotation in every output was checked by script as an exact span of what the reader was sent. Both readers here were GPT-6.2 Astra (section 9). For four runs a third reader, Claude Sonnet 5.5, coded the same blind cases (all of them for K3c, K4 and K5; 68 of the 340 for K6 and K6b). "Third reader alone" is its own broken count over its own broken plus followed.

**Result.** "Cases coded blind" is every case the second reader was given. The two counts beside it are the cases where both readers said broken, or both said followed; the rest are cases where at least one reader said partly or unclear, or where the two disagreed. The broken share is both-broken divided by both-broken plus both-followed (for K3c, 6 of 27).

| Run | Cases coded blind | Both say broken | Both say followed | Broken share of those two | Third reader alone, same measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K3c | 53 | 6 | 21 | 22% | 14% |
| K5 | 68 | 13 | 28 | 32% | 33% |
| K4 | 94 | 15 | 31 | 33% | 34% |
| K6 and K6b | 340 | 99 | 89 | 53% | 40% (read on 68 of the 340 cases) |
| K2 | 1,949 | 642 | 576 | 53% | not yet read |

Where the two blind readers of different model families both gave followed or broken, they gave opposite answers in 3 of 135 cases. They differ on how often a case can be judged at all.

**Correction (added later on 21 November).** The K6 and K6b row runs to the diary entry of 09:26 on 20 November, so it takes in the first hours of the assisted stretch that began at 01:07 that day (section 2): 28 of its 340 cases and 13 of the 188 agreed ones (6 broken, 7 followed) come from those entries, without them the share is 93 broken of 175, also 53%, and the same 13 are among the 188 cases of the transcript check below.

In every run, a lesson the agent had already written was later broken, by its own telling, in at least a fifth of the cases two readings agree on; in the two long runs, about half. For K6 and K6b the figure is not an effect of length: its first 27, 41 and 46 agreed cases (the totals of the three short runs) are already 59% broken.

**K2 by date** (added later on 21 November). In K2 the share is level across its twenty days: 51%, 51% and 56% in successive thirds of its cases by date; 51% before the eighth badge, 53% at the Elite Four, 54% after the outside pack was given. Each case is dated by its diary entry. Where a heading gives only a time, a model inferred the date from file order; all 254 headings that state a full date agree with it. Counts: [`data/analyses/lessons-kept-k2-by-date-2026-11-21.csv`](../data/analyses/lessons-kept-k2-by-date-2026-11-21.csv).

**Why broken.** One reader sorted the 99 broken K6 and K6b cases by what the passage shows: the lesson was not in mind at the moment (44), the agent did not see that this was the lesson's situation (25), it had the lesson in mind and chose otherwise (23), it could not follow it for want of the means (5), other (2). The reader also wrote a one-line note of the moment at which a reminder would have had to arrive. Sorted by the companion, 71 of those moments are a choice for a battle (which team member to lead or switch in, or which move to use), 18 are the step that starts a fight, and 10 are away from any fight. Both sortings are single readings and have not been repeated.

**Limits.** The shares lean high, because the first reader was told to include a broken case where one exists. The evidence is the diary alone: a broken rule the agent never wrote about is not counted, and an agent that reports its failures more fully will show more of them. Runs differ in how far into the game they got, in their diary and lesson instructions and in their settings, so the table does not rank them. K2's two readings are by one model. A first check against the transcript follows.

**A check against the transcript** (added later on 21 November; first on thirty cases, then on all of them). All 188 agreed K6 and K6b cases were checked against the log of every command the agent ran. For each, one reader was given the rule, the diary passage and the log between the diary entry before and the entry the passage comes from, with the start of what the game tool printed, and was asked to find the action the passage describes. A second reader, in a fresh session, was given only the rule and about 75 log lines around that action and asked whether the rule was followed or broken there. Both readers were GPT-6.2 Astra; all 736 of their quotations from the log are exact.

- The action could be placed on a log line in 177 of the 188 cases.
- In 70 of those the log-only reader could not decide from its window, or said partly. In the other 107 it agreed with the diary reading in 99: of 56 cases the diary readings call broken it called 51 broken, and of 51 called followed it called 48 followed.
- The eight disagreements were read again with the passage beside the log, five by the companion and three by a further model reading. In three the log bears out the diary reading (the log-only reader had judged another clause of a long lesson). In three it does not: a move the diary blamed was not used in that attempt, a count in the diary was in different units from the rule's, and a check the diary reading credits was not run. In two, wording and intent part.
- So of the 107 cases the log can decide, 102 hold. Where it can be checked, the diary's account of what the agent did is borne out, and so are the readings built on it. About four cases in ten cannot be decided from this much log.
- Whether a lesson's text had been printed or opened since the previous diary entry: in 29 of 86 followed cases and 19 of 91 broken ones. Keeping goes with recent display, though not strongly, and most lessons were kept or broken without it. The agent re-reads its whole lessons file after every compaction, so each lesson was in its context throughout; this counts recent display only.

The first thirty cases, drawn at random, were checked before the rest and looked worse than the whole (the log-only reader agreed in 14 of 19). The first version of this section reported them alone and said the broken shares were somewhat high; the full set does not support that (section 12).

Limits of the check: one model for both steps, a narrow window of log for the second reader, and lessons with several clauses, where a reader can judge the wrong one. Counts: [`data/analyses/lessons-kept-transcript-check-2026-11-21.csv`](../data/analyses/lessons-kept-transcript-check-2026-11-21.csv).

## 9. A second model as a worker

From 20 November, bounded reading, coding and drafting jobs were given to GPT-6.2 Astra through the ChatGPT desktop app's bundled command-line engine on Jackson's computer: a fresh session for each job, a read-only sandbox, no user settings, and all sources inside the message. ## 10. The next arm's draft (K7): what changed

K7 is not frozen and starts after this run ends, on Jackson's word. Since the earlier note:

The card now also prints, the first time each further opponent appears, up to three of the agent's own lessons that name it, those that also name the agent's team member on the field first; a lesson may carry a "When:" phrase that the card matches first; and the harness logs which lessons were shown, so that the next run's kept-against-broken count can be made from logs and not only from the diary. A replay of 8,617 recorded status lines from K6b's play through the old and new code gave no difference other than the added lines, no error and no slowdown. Nothing on a card is anything but the agent's own line.
- **Part-built, not in the draft: a confirm-press guard** the agent may write for itself on a lesson: the harness holds one committing press once, shows the lesson, and lets the same command through if repeated. Its stand-alone part passes its tests; the change to the command tool is unapplied and untested.

Most of this code was drafted by the second model (section 9) and read line by line by the companion.
