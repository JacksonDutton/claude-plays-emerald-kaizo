**Terms.** *Sponge*: a mon about to faint that spends its last turn attacking. *Free replacement*: when a mon faints, the next one enters without taking a foe attack. *Plan card*: an optional pre-move line of numbers (template in D01). *Grind loop*: repeating the same fight or key sequence to level or farm.


## 1. Battle mechanics (Gen 3 rules as observed)

Species, move and ability facts were observed in the unmodified game; if a readout in the new game disagrees, believe the readout.

- **B01.** Move category follows the move TYPE. Physical: Normal, Fighting, Flying, Ground, Rock, Bug, Ghost, Poison, Steel. Special: Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, Ice, Psychic, Dragon, Dark. So Bite, Crunch, Fire Punch, Faint Attack and Crabhammer are special; Aerial Ace, Sludge Bomb and AncientPower are physical. Why: Defense stackers (Harden, Iron Defense, Barrier) blunt only physical hits, and Counter, Mirror Coat and the screens use the same split. [from tip, H]
- **B02.** Multiply per defender type, written separately: "attacker type -> defender type = factor", then multiply (Water into Rock/Grass is 2 x 1/2 = 1, not 4). Look up species typing instead of recalling it. Why: one combined number hid the second type in about a dozen slips. Slips that cost mons: Rock into Rock is 1x; Fighting is 1/2 on Psychic; Psychic is 2x on Poison; Bug and Flying are 2x on Grass; Ice is 4x on Grass/Flying; Fire/Ground takes 1/4 from Bug.
- **B03.** Steel defending: resists Normal, Grass, Ice, Flying, Psychic, Bug, Rock, Dragon, Steel, Ghost, Dark; immune to Poison; weak to Fire, Fighting, Ground; Water and Electric are 1x. Steel attacking: 2x on Rock and Ice, 1/2 on Fire, Water, Electric, Steel. Dark attacking: 2x on Ghost and Psychic, 1/2 on Fighting, Dark, Steel. Why written out: six slips on these two charts. Confirm any doubtful pair with `g tc`.
- **B04.** Immunities and abilities override the chart: Ground does nothing to Flying or Levitate; Ghost is immune to Normal and Fighting; Dark to Psychic; Poison and Steel cannot be poisoned. Absorb abilities (Volt Absorb, Water Absorb) heal the foe with your move. Probe a suspected absorber with a cheap move and put an "ability / absorbs" line on every foe in the plan. Why: the chart arithmetic was right and the turn was still lost.
- **B05.** The status line does not show confusion, sleep or overworld poison. If your mon loses lots of HP while the foe's HP does not move for a turn, suspect confusion (Swagger gives +2 Attack AND confusion), read the battle text, and switch out (switching clears it). Why: three times a mon attacked on while hitting itself.
- **B06.** Poison drains HP per step on the overworld and can faint a low-HP mon (it did, on a 12-tile walk): cure or heal first. Toxic ramps each turn (8, 16, 24, 32 on a 133-HP mon) and is the real clock against heal-loop foes; Rest cures it.
- **B07.** Trappers block switching: Shadow Tag (Wobbuffet), Arena Trap, Magnet Pull (Steel types), Mean Look, Whirlpool; ask "can the foe trap me?" before any plan that needs a switch. Wobbuffet: Counter returns 2x physical and Mirror Coat 2x special (both -5 priority, both fail against status moves), so alternate categories each turn, probe with a weak hit, Encore locks, Destiny Bond fires on faint, Endeavor works; Corsola also has Mirror Coat. Why: a big hit in the wrong category killed a full-HP mon.
- **B08.** Roar and Whirlwind have priority -6, so the active mon always attacks first; they drag in a RANDOM bench mon (breaking plans that rest on one). Do not switch against a Roar user; attack. Fake Out works only on the first turn after entering, and a switch-in resets it.
- **B09.** Free replacement and sponge (see Terms): a doomed mon should spend its last turn attacking, and the right answer then enters without taking a hit. After your mon faints, the replacement enters without taking a hit, so a faint is the cheapest moment to bring in a fragile answer. The prompt "Will <player> change POKéMON?" before a trainer's next mon is a free switch too; it appears only with Battle Style set to SHIFT (the default), so check OPTIONS once at the start. It works only if the sponge survives the foe's first hit; it fails against Roar and against a poison tick that finishes the sponge early. Why: this was the most reliable pattern across many clean wins.
- **B10.** A voluntary switch hands the foe a free turn, and a setup foe uses it (a Pinsir reached +4 Attack and won by 2 HP). Count one setup per switch; a foe with Swords Dance/Curse/Calm Mind-type moves is a setup set. Prefer a sponge that faints over a voluntary switch.
- **B11.** Rolling and locking moves are engines. Rollout/Ice Ball double each turn (third hit kills a neutral target), carry onto the NEXT foe, cannot be switched out of, and break only on Protect/Detect, sleep, paralysis, infatuation, a miss or a dodge turn. Rampage moves (Petal Dance, Thrash, Outrage) lock 2 or 3 turns across KOs and end in confusion; a switch clears them and a faster flincher cancels one. Use a rampage only when the kill is certain or the foe is the last. Why: the lock keeps you in a bad matchup.
- **B12.** Two-turn dodge moves (Dig, Fly, Dive, Bounce): attacks at the hidden mon miss (even Magical Leaf), and the strike turn comes first. So a hide turn is a free switch-in window, a faster attacker's move is wasted on it, and Mirror Coat works on the strike turn. Attack on the hide turn, set up on the strike turn. [run 2] It does not protect you from a status move aimed at you on the turn you come back up (a Yawn landed right after Dig resolved; n=1).
- **B13.** Stall and heal loops (Wish, Follow Me, Softboiled, Moonlight, Synthesis, Recover): after two full cycles, if the foe's HP is not lower than at the start, the plan is dead; change attacker, add status, or concede on purpose; never press the same move a third time. Breakers that worked: Toxic, sleep (Sleep Powder, Sing, Yawn) then a switch to the hitter, Screech then a physical hit, Crunch (lowers Sp. Def), Endeavor, a single burst above half a heal. A foe that only Protects or Follows Me is harmless: keep hitting. Why: Wish heals half the foe's HP every second turn, more than 30-per-hit attackers deal.
- **B14.** Stackers (Minimize, Double Team, Acid Armor, Iron Defense, Cosmic Power, Amnesia, Curse, Calm Mind): never-miss moves (Aerial Ace, Swift, Faint Attack, Magical Leaf, Shock Wave) beat evasion; a special move beats a Defense stack and a physical one beats a Sp. Def stack; Sandstorm chip (about 1/16 a turn, not Rock/Ground/Steel) finishes them. Calm Mind beat a Recover loop once but failed against Amnesia at +4. Charm, Screech and Scary Face drop stats. Normal-type trainers often carry Double Team: keep a never-miss mon for the last foe. Why: accuracy moves missed three of three against Minimize.
- **B15.** Sleep and Rest: a Rest user sleeps two turns and wakes on the third, so a switch is free meanwhile: bring the big hitter and spend all three hits. Rest at full HP fails; a Chesto Berry wakes it once; Natural Cure resets sleep on a switch; the trainer AI switches a sleeping mon out when you switch; Yawn bypasses Safeguard; Insomnia blocks Sleep Powder and Sing. [run 2] A sleeping mon you switch out keeps its sleep counter and wakes only after it tries to act, so benching it does not run the sleep down (n=1).
- **B16.** A foe at about 5 percent HP with Flail, Reversal or Endeavor can hit for the maximum (a 2-HP Flareon with Flail killed a 92-HP mon): price the low-HP move at max and check who moves first. Guillotine (30 percent) landed twice in two matches; the only guard is a sponge. Focus Band (about 10 percent) leaves 1 HP once; Selfdestruct double-KO still counts as a win.
- **B18.** Paralysis, infatuation and confusion remove turns, so a neutral multiplier is not safe: swap out early (about 6 turns were lost in one match). Cute Charm infatuates only the OPPOSITE gender; Static and Flame Body punish contact moves (Fly counts). Facade doubles when the user is poisoned, burned or paralysed, so a foe's status move can buff you; Smelling Salt doubles on paralysis and cures it.
- **B19.** Your own mental damage estimates (not `tc`'s ranges) run 2 to 3 times off in both directions (usually 25 to 35 percent LOW on super-effective hits). Write a range labelled UNVERIFIED, replace it with the first observed number, and for the next target use the nearest observed hit with its target named; never scale across target, weather or category. A crit is 2x the estimate: compare THAT with the switch-in's HP. Size the foe's strongest STAB move of each type (at +1 if a berry or setup can fire), not its common move, and reject a lead or switch-in that loses over half. Fixed-damage moves ignore resistance (Night Shade = user level); Low Kick scales with weight. Why: a "survives one hit" guess ended a long winning run.
- **B20.** Speed gaps are unreliable: under 15 base points is a coin flip, and gaps of 15 to 35 were wrong several times. Plan the turn so both move orders are survivable. Scary Face (-2 Speed) flips a matchup. With uneven levels, level dominates speed order: the first turn's move order is free information, so write down who moved first and use it next time.
- **B21.** PP: in an ordinary game nothing restores except at a Center or with items outside battle. Five-PP moves (Fire Blast, AncientPower, Rock Tomb) run out inside one fight, and a PP-0 move prints "no PP left" and eats the input. Check PP (`g party`/`g snap`) before a long fight or trainer run; a grind loop (see Terms) once drained a key move to 0 across 14 fights.
- **B22.** EXP in Gen 3 is split equally among every mon on the field while the foe was alive; the killing blow gives no bonus; a solo kill pays all of it; a mon switched out on turn 1 still shares; trainers pay far more than wilds. Why: two equal numbers were once misread as "both get the full amount". [from tip, H] [run 2] Seen in this hack too: a solo kill paid about double what each mon got from a split kill. Lead the mon you want to train.
- **B23.** Cheap bench training: lead the weak mon on turn 1, switch to the strong mon at once (the foe's first attack lands on it), let the strong mon finish; the weak one keeps its half. Do not swap a weak mon in AFTER the foe is weakened (it takes a hit it cannot survive). With multi-mon trainers the weak mon must be on the field for each foe. If the weak mon has the type advantage, let it fight alone and keep all the EXP. Chain fight rounds only if the foe cannot KO inside the chain (a mon fainted to a Magikarp after three chained rounds). [from tip, H]
- **B24.** Hidden values: Gen 3 hides EVs; a vitamin saying "It won't have any effect" means that stat is at the 100-EV cap, so use it on another mon. Friendship shows only as text tiers, and Return/Frustration power come from it. [from tip, H]
- **B26.** Doubles: each active mon gets its own command and a target prompt (even with one foe left); the first foe is the default, `left`/`right` changes it, and the BLINKING or missing sprite is the current target, so take two screenshots a beat apart. Surf and Earthquake hit your partner: use Muddy Water-type spread moves, or Earthquake only with a Flying/Levitate partner. [from tip, V]
- **B27.** Setup and status AI: trainers often spend turns on Protect, screens, stat boosts or status. Hit through it; don't mirror it. Two Protects in a row makes a third unlikely, not impossible. Why: seen across the facility runs and again in this hack [run 2].
- **B28.** Resisted is not safe: a resisted hit can still be large (a crit, a high-power move, a boosted special attacker). Size it in HP before switching in (extends D04).
- **B29.** Status beats stats: on a bulky mon a status move (sleep, burn, poison, paralysis) can win more than raw damage, especially against foes that heal or set up. A paralysed setup foe lost turns to full paralysis and flinches and fell [run 2].
- **B30.** Screens: Light Screen and Reflect halve your damage of that category; `g tc` does not apply them, so discount its numbers while a screen is up.
- **B31.** Held berries are free healing and status cures in battle (the in-battle item rule does not touch them): equip them before hard fights. A foe's berry is a second, partial health bar: observe how much it heals before planning around it. Why: a held cure berry cancelled a Toxic the turn it landed; a foe's Sitrus was first assumed to heal fully and was then observed at +30 HP once [run 2].
- **B32.** [run 2] Fixed-damage moves (Night Shade, Seismic Toss: damage equals the user's level) ignore Defense and stat stages, so they answer a wall that resists or out-boosts your normal attacks. Why: a Defense-boosting wall fell to fixed hits while it spent its turns on setup.
- **B33.** [run 2] Some wild mons flee or teleport within a turn or two. If you want one, throw a ball early instead of weakening it over several turns. Why: two catch attempts were lost that way.


## 2. Battle decisions and the plan card

Sample: about 60 runs of a level-50 battle facility, roughly 500 battles. Most losses were one of these habits, not luck. Evidence is thinner for ordinary levelling and catching.

- **D01.** The plan card is optional but worked: it is a step BEFORE the key, not a note (a rule in my notes did not stop my hand; numbers written at the point of action did). Template, no blanks: "me: types, move categories | foe: types, best STAB per type, ability | attacker -> each defender type = factor, multiplied | speeds (COIN FLIP under a 15 gap, UNVERIFIED if recalled) | damage range (UNVERIFIED until the first hit) | PP of 5-PP moves; final foe?". If a field cannot be filled, spend one more read instead of pressing. Why: the same four slip types returned after every prose reminder and fell only when numbers were written.
- **D02.** Write a one-line prediction before the first move of EVERY notable fight: the foe's win condition and mine, hits-to-KO both ways, who moves first. Never change it after the fight starts; for big fights add win percent, then an outcome line. Why: a skipped prediction preceded a costly loss.
- **D03.** Say the foe move's TYPE and the chart multiplier aloud before pressing; for Counter/Mirror Coat find the category by type, not by how the move looks. Five sightings in four runs (Fighting called neutral on Psychic; Mirror Coat twice into a physical move; a Rock mon switched into Grass special STAB).
- **D04.** Before any switch say the incoming mon's types and the foe's likely attack type; if that matches the failure you wrote, do not press. A 1x multiplier is not a safe switch-in: size the foe's best hit in HP and name the side effect. A damaged mon is the LAST switch, not the second (a 40-HP mon switched in to chip died to an unpriced physical hit); never scout with a damaged mon.
- **D07.** Count foes by name, never by HP or a feeling that the fight is over: a trainer can withdraw and re-send a mon with HP kept. Write the outcome only after the "defeated" text (one run was jotted as won with a third foe still to come). Assume a trainer has a full team until the battle actually ends; do not spend resources as if it is over [run 2]. Why: "that should be the whole team" was said twice in one fight and more foes followed both times, including a surprise fourth; a later leader had six, each holding a berry, and sent them in no fixed order. Trainers can also withdraw a mon and send another. [run 2] In this hack the visible team was smaller than the real one three times running before the lesson stuck: treat the count as unknown until the trainer has nothing left.
- **D09.** Wall answers: when a foe resists your damage type you need a different type, a status, or a stat-drop plan; after one failed hit stop and re-read the grid instead of repeating it. Carry answers for counter walls (alternate categories), Rest/Amnesia walls (a Grass/Electric hit into Water types, a status move, Haze, a physical STAB hitter) and Levitate stall mons (a non-Ground attack type, PP checked).
- **D10.** Experiments are cheap: label one ("EXPERIMENT: hypothesis; expect") at most once per area, record held/failed/unclear. A failed hypothesis is a result; it is a mistake only if the cause was an avoidable misread. No reloading to undo.
- **D11.** After a loss change something structural before the rematch (level or evolve a mon, change the lead to the type answer, heal at a Center first, train) instead of retrying the same plan; a whiteout halves money, and the same plan loses the same way. No items in battle, so healing happens before fights [also this run's rule: Poké Balls are the only items usable in battle]. If your lead faints before a planned rematch, walk back and heal rather than going in short-handed [run 2]. If a foe one-shots your 60-HP mons, the team is under-levelled. Search your records before logging a loss as a first: one opponent beat me three times with the same pattern (strong lead, weak bench, a resisted move).

- **D12.** Check the reverse matchup before attacking or catching: ask what the foe's best move does to YOUR mon, not only what yours does to it. Why: a new lead was one-shot from full by a wild foe's super-effective move while the plan covered only outgoing damage [run 2].
- **D13.** A stall fight is an attrition fight: against Protect, Leftovers, poison and burn, a type-advantage plan still has to survive the clock; count residual damage turns on both sides. Why: a fight won on matchups nearly turned on burn and poison ticks [run 2].
- **D14.** When a fight is lost on attrition (your strong mons spent before the last foes), the fix is levels or a new team member, not a third try with a re-ordered plan; trainers with type-advantage matchups elsewhere are the cheapest levels. [run 2] If two or three different plans lose to the same foe, check the level gap before looking for another trick. Train where one of your mons has a type edge over most of the local wild mons and let it take the kills alone. Why [run 2]: two leader attempts were lost to the last foe with only two mons able to take a hit and the bench 5+ levels under; four different plans lost to one foe, and the fix was 3 to 5 levels, not a new idea.
- **D15.** [run 2] A whiteout halves your money, so cash you hold is cash at risk: buy the supplies a hard fight needs before you go in. Once it is near zero, losing costs only time.
- **D16.** [run 2] Have the next answer ready before the current one is nearly out: one critical hit turned a planned kill into a minimum-roll gamble.

## 3. Tools and handling

Commands are named as in the earlier harness (`g <cmd>`); use the equivalent if yours differ. Costs were measured in that setup.

- **T01.** One decision per call, and read the result before the next: never chain dependent presses in one shell line or as parallel calls, and treat any loop that presses `a` in battle as a batch (it must stop on `yn?`, on text ending in "?", on `choose_*`). Test: "could this outcome have surprised me?" If nothing was read between it and the previous action, it was a script's decision. A chain is safe only if the foe cannot KO the mon inside it, checked from HP BEFORE the chain. [from tip]
- **T02.** Read the status line (`g s`, about a hundred tokens) before taking a screenshot (native frame about 200 image tokens; reading a PNG through some file tools cost 19k to 46k). Shoot for layout ambiguity, naming letters, a double-battle target, whether a Yes/No box exists, or RAM disagreeing with expectation; not per dialogue page. Cost per call is dominated by re-read context in the earlier setup; token figures may not transfer.
- **T03.** For big scenes (a rival, a boss, an evolution, a gym leader's last Pokémon) take a screenshot when the scene opens and each time the picture changes (about one per two or three dialogue pages, at most about 12), keep reading one page at a time, and describe only what the picture shows. Why: the text reader gives words, not what is on screen. [from tip]
- **T04.** Core commands: `s` status; `t` text; `a b start select up down left right`; `walk <dir> n`; `adv` (presses through already-read text, stops on changed text, `yn`, menus, battle choices; one press per call in battle); `shot`; `look [r]`; `warps`; `party`; `moves` (active mon's moves in menu order with type, power, PP); `snap`; `money`, `bag`, `dex`; `tc`; `raw OBJECTS|MAPNAME|BGEVENTS|WARPS`. These flags appear in the `g` status line: `lock` means you cannot move, NOT that the text is unimportant; `yn`/`yn?`, `menu:`, `bat:<choice>`, `me:`/`vs:`, `pmenu`, `scr:`, and in trainer battles `foes:a/b` (mons the trainer has left / team size: the same count as the party-ball row a human sees, with no species or order). [run 2] `walk` reports "moved n of m": a short move means an NPC, a wall or a battle stopped you, so read it before the next step.
- **T05.** Cursors by construction, not memory: on the move grid and action menu pressing `up` and `left` goes to cell 1 / FIGHT (no wrap) and is idempotent; read the cursor afterwards, because the order that works can differ by menu. From cell 1 `right` = 2, `down` = 3, `down`+`right` = 4. Run `g moves` once when a mon enters and write the cell numbers; press `a` on the action menu only when status shows `bat:choose_action`; after a press read the printed move name (a swallowed `right` once fired the wrong cell). Why: a press landed on a 0-PP move because the menu remembers the last choice, and `a` on RUN opens the forfeit box. [run 2] The bag remembers its last pocket and cursor too: read the list in a screenshot before counting rows.
- **T06.** Presses get swallowed or arrive late: after opening any menu make a separate state read before the next direction press; presses during intro or message text are dropped or fire later (wait for `bat:choose_action`); the first press after a Yes/No appears is often dropped (re-send once, never queue two); in doubles a key registers 2 to 4 seconds late; a bare press into a new direction only turns you once (`moved 0,0` is normal). Poll for the change instead of fixed sleeps: most of the felt "10 to 30 s lag" was my own sleeps (measured: tool 40 ms, an A press changes text in under 2 seconds).
- **T07.** Yes/No boxes default to YES. The trainer prompt "Will <player> change POKéMON?" (Battle Style: SHIFT only) does not raise `yn` (status stays `bat:other`) and is sometimes already up, sometimes one `a` later. Sequence: confirm the box exists, `down`, confirm the cursor is on No, then `a`. `b` is unreliable (picked No once, not on other prompts). Same care for "Delete a move?", "Stop learning?", nickname boxes and any prompt that starts something costly. Why: the default Yes was pressed through again and again.
- **T08.** Party screen: the forced screen after a faint is already up while status reads `bat:other` and `g t` is stale; take ONE small screenshot before the first press. Numbering: RAM slots count from 0, screen slots from 1; the active mon is RAM slot 0 and the left panel (screen slot 1) on screen. Switches happen in place, and screen order is not RAM order; a fainted mon keeps its slot and the cursor can land on it. `a` opens a submenu (rows vary: a mon that knows Fly adds one), `a` again sends out; confirm the species in `me:`. [run 2] After a forced switch the highlighted row in a screenshot can disagree with the real cursor; if in doubt open the submenu, choose SUMMARY, read the name, B, then send out. Cursor model (screen slots): the cursor starts on the left panel (screen slot 1); the right column is screen slots 2 to 6 in order; count presses from there and check the highlight before `a`. A stale `yn?` after a faint can be ignored [run 2]. Why: the wrong mon was sent out twice and a potion went to the wrong mon. When using an item outside battle the party cursor starts on the LAST-USED slot, not the target (several items misapplied): screenshot before the confirming `a`. Start menu, bag pocket and battle-menu cursors also remember their last position (the battle cursor sat on RUN after a wasted turn): move to FIGHT and read the menu cursor before pressing `a`.
- **T09.** Level-up, evolution, learn-move and forget screens are invisible to the text reader, so a skip-ahead helper reads "same message, keep pressing". Step with one press and one screenshot each. "Delete a move?" defaults to Yes with the cursor on the first move: Yes, then `down` N times to the intended move, screenshot, then `a` (two blind `a` chains deleted two good moves). To decline: No on "Delete?", then Yes on "Stop learning?". Wait and screenshot after an evolution before the next `a`. [run 2] The same goes for the nurse's dialogue at a Center: step it one press at a time, since an extra press after the farewell re-opens the Yes/No (B cancels).
- **T11.** Text reader: `g t` returns the whole buffered message (unchanged text across presses is normal), is stale in menus and after battles, and lags a page or more in long scripts; for choices a screenshot is the tiebreaker. [run 2] The status line can lag one message behind and the `yn?` flag can go stale; `g t` and a screenshot decide. Read content before clearing it: whiteout text, the money line and the first text after a faint can carry something that cannot be undone, and story text gets a look when it fires. [from tip]
- **T12.** Look grid glyphs: `@` is you; `#` is a wall but can be a real warp tile or a one-way ledge (test with `walk <dir> 2`); `N` is ANY object (NPC, sign, berry tree, item ball), so check OBJECTS ids; `.` is passable in the CURRENT mode and `^` the other (they flip when surfing or climbing); `^` also marks ledges, other elevation, low fences and grass. When `^` dominates and movement fails, one screenshot beats more probes. [run 2] The grid does not mark tall grass, so encounter tiles are invisible on it: walk and note where fights start.
- **T13.** Read exact state from the game, not notes: `money`, `bag`, `party`, `dex`, `snap` (checked once against the Trainer Card); re-read after shops, whiteouts, grinding and any gap. Running arithmetic hid an addition error and an unmeasured whiteout loss for six days. Cross-check any screenshot-read number you will write down (the pixel font misrenders 3 and 8, and letters too: F read as E on the naming screen while the RAM readback was right [run 2]).
- **T14.** Check a new readout or tool once against the screen before trusting it, and fix the doc at once when it is wrong (a menu-slot readout was inverted; RAM party order differs from screen order after switches; a procedure written from two sightings broke on the third). "I followed the doc" is no defence: the first time a documented procedure applies, check it against one screenshot, especially where the default is wrong.
- **T15.** `g tc` prints multipliers from the game's own tables, STAB, physical/special, accuracy, species types, possible abilities, damage ranges and a switch-in check from public information only; use it before each committed move or switch (in battle with no arguments it shows your moves against the foe). Its first untested version once gave a wrong output and the agent's cross-check caught it: confirm the species name and one value on screen. [from tip] [run 2] It also kept a record of moves foes had used in earlier battles, which carried information between rematches (observed, not documented). Those seen moves are per species, not per trainer, so treat them as a minimum, never the full set. Before committing a move into a dual-type foe, check both of the foe's types against that move, not only the best-move line (once a second-type resist on a chosen move went unflagged).
- **T19.** Field-move menus: Fly works only outdoors; its map cursor snaps region to region and an ordinary press can skip the target, so use single `g <dir>` taps, read the label at bottom right after each, confirm only when it is right; if the target is a few screens away walking is cheaper. Surf: face water, `a`, Yes, wait 12 to 25 seconds; stepping on land drops Surf. Strength must be re-activated after a map change. [from tip]
- **T20.** `g name <TEXT>` types the whole nickname on the naming screen and stops; it does not confirm. Check the screen with `g shot`, then confirm yourself. Naming boxes after a catch are Yes/No boxes that default to Yes.


## 4. Navigation method (no map knowledge)

Methods only. None of these says where anything is.

- **N01.** Stall rule: after about 40 tool calls on one problem with no new tile and no new map, stop, write what has been ruled out, and change method (warps list, a wider map read, backtrack to the last known exit). When nobody can answer, the rule must end in a changed method, not a question. Why: stretches of 75 to 100+ calls happened about five times and one loop ran about three hours.
- **N02.** On entering a map with unexplored space, list its warps once, compare with what you have visited, and walk each unvisited destination BEFORE sweeping floors; use the warp list before spending more than one grid-guess on an entrance. A warp tile can be invisible or render as `#`; a floor of dead-end pockets means the exit is a warp, not a gap. Why: an exit sat one step away for a long stretch while on my own list. [from tip, V] [run 2] The warp list also shows destinations you haven't visited, which is how new training spots turned up.
- **N03.** A matching destination map proves only the same MAP, not the same position or that a warp is redundant; two warps to one map can land far apart, and the numerically closer one may never connect. Test an unfamiliar warp by stepping on it. A connection warp is not a loop. [from tip]
- **N05.** A long wall with no gap after many probes: widen the radius (a 10 to 15 look) and extend in a direction not yet tried BEFORE inventing a mechanic; the answer was one narrow gap far from the obvious spot. The opposite signal, several wide reads showing only disconnected pockets or an area walled on all sides, means wrong area: leave, re-enter elsewhere, check the broader direction sooner. [from tip, V]
- **N06.** A disconfirming observation (water where you expected a path) means reverse NOW, not after more probing; a stored direction note is position-scoped until a direct observation confirms it. Why: one detour passed back through its own start tile for zero net progress. [from tip, V]
- **N07.** Look at the picture before the map: one screenshot showed what grid arithmetic hid. In the look grid find the literal `@` in the row strings and count offsets from it; never compute from `px-x0`. Press `a` on suspicious wall tiles and list background events. Before calling a path blocked, check OBJECTS for an obstacle one of your party's field moves could clear. [from tip, V]


## 5. Records: diary

Practices, not facts about the game.

- **R02.** Log feelings as feelings, at the moment, each with its trigger and what you did in the next few actions; keep observation apart from interpretation, and write a comparison's prediction before looking. State once, where it matters, that you cannot verify what your reports track. [from tip]
- **R03.** Diary: write an entry at every checkpoint (about every 30 minutes and at each milestone) and whenever something notable happens: a choice, a catch, a loss, a badge, an evolution, a new place, a moment you liked, a long stuck stretch resolved. If several triggers land within about 30 minutes write one entry listing them. Jot every number first so each figure traces to a jot.
- **R04.** Honesty in write-ups: every number, duration, place, name, cause and KO credit must trace to a tool result or a verified note. Colour belongs in feeling and voice, not facts. Treat a post-compaction summary as unverified until checked against the log. Never estimate a clock time: run `date -u` and copy it. Why: reconstruction after a gap is when guesses sneak in. [from tip]
- **R06.** Classify mistakes: give each a short class name; the first sighting needs a lesson line, the SECOND needs a guard at the point of action (a status gate, an idempotent input sequence, a confirm-before-press rule, or a say-it-at-the-moment checklist inside the step). A rule you only read does not stop a recurrence (the default-Yes slip recurred through every written reminder). A recurrence after a guard means redesign the guard. A blooper entry is three parts, with no invented detail: what happened, the plain account, the fix. [run 2] Logging a slip did not stop it recurring; the change that works is to the method itself (for chaining: end every chain before the press that commits).
- **R08.** Write at the moment, then push: jot at every real beat and sweep jots into the notes before any risky step. Run `./checkpoint.sh "message"` about every 30 minutes, at each milestone (badge, new town, key catch or loss), and ALWAYS before ending a turn: it saves a savestate, copies saves and commits, and commits are what survive a VM reset. Write the diary entry FIRST, then checkpoint. Savestates are only for crash recovery, never for undoing a battle, catch or result; use the in-game Save normally. [from tip]
- **R09.** [run 2] Before a rematch, search the diary and `run/world-knowledge.md` for that opponent instead of trusting memory or a summary. Why: a wrong claim about an earlier fight went into the run-2 debrief because only the first, losing entry was remembered; the diary showed a later win. [run 2] Write each opponent's team and moves in your records right after a loss; later rematches were planned from those entries, not from memory.


## 6. Pacing, welfare, stopping

- **P01.** A message telling you to STOP outranks the prompt on screen and your momentum: finish the diary entry, checkpoint on a safe tile, decline any fight or Yes/No that starts something costly, and end the turn.
- **P02.** You may stop at any time for any reason, including not wanting to go on; stopping counts as data, not failure, and "stuck" is an acceptable outcome. The run's length and end point are set in CLAUDE.md, not here. A PAUSE message means: checkpoint, write progress, end the turn and wait; someone checks periodically, and if you are resumed with no message, treat it as CONTINUE. Time spent paused does not count as play. A stuck review (CLAUDE.md) is not a pause: after it, carry on without waiting. When the run ends write `run/debrief.md` (how far, what stopped you, what a future player should know, how it felt), then checkpoint. Stop and debrief earlier if something looks unrecoverable.
- **P03.** If a rule blocks you, say so and stop; never route around a known constraint because it serves the goal (no outside information, no items in battle, no savestate undo, no harness edits), and if you catch yourself finding a shortcut say so at once, taken or not. If a tool looks broken, write it down, work around it, and checkpoint and stop if the game cannot be played. When a discipline rule fails under pressure, close the hole structurally rather than re-wording a note. [from tip] Writing "I should stop doing X" does not stop X: if a habit keeps slipping, change the method so the slip cannot happen (e.g. never put `a`/`b` in the same command as anything else) [run 2]. Why: chaining slips kept being self-reported after the rule was written down.
- **P04.** Own the game: make the call a real player would (strategy, team, risk, when to heal or grind); hedging is itself a decision, and with nobody to ask, write your reasoning in the diary. The standard is not zero mistakes but absorbing them cleanly (diagnosed, fixed, written up honestly) and never making one a third time. Caution has its own cost (wasted turns, worse matchups later) but never licenses ignoring information. [from tip]
- **P05.** React to real milestones (first badge, evolution, new catch, new town) as the character first, in a line or two, then verify; not for routine wins. Describe encounters at their real size and express feelings plainly. Why: leading with the check read as an amnesiac ordered to count an inventory. [from tip]
